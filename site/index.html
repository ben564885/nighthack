The problem

AI writes the instructions. Nobody checks the wires.

More people build electronics from chatbot instructions every week. A chatbot cannot see the board. One crossed jumper can fry a sensor, or start a fire on a desk.

01

It cannot see

The model describes a circuit. It has no way to know what you actually plugged in.

02

You find out late

The first sign of a short is smoke, a hot chip, or a dead USB port.

03

Beginners pay most

The people who need guidance most are the people least able to spot a bad wire.

How it works

Scan first. Power second.

Two multiplexers watch sixteen rows. One drives a safe test voltage into a row. The other reads every other row. A voltage on another row means the two rows are connected. The whole board maps in a fraction of a second, with the power off.

test voltageconnection foundmatches the plan
STEP 1

Scan

The Arduino maps which rows touch. The scan only runs while the power is off, so the test voltage never meets a live circuit.

STEP 2

Compare

The map is checked against the circuit plan. Rails touching, stray links and missing jumpers each get a plain fix: "remove the wire between row 5 and row 6".

STEP 3

Power

Claude calls power_on. The board refuses if the check fails. If it passes, power flows through a current sensor and a switch.

Safety

The board decides. The AI never does.

The AI helps you build. It is never in the safety path. A bad answer, a slow network or a closed laptop cannot keep power on.

01

The Arduino cuts power by itself. It reads current and temperature all the time. Over the limit, it opens the switch. No laptop, no network.

02

Power starts off, always. After a reset or a crash, the switch is open before any other code runs.

03

No scan while live. The multiplexers are disabled before power-on, so the scan layer cannot disturb a running circuit.

04

No remote power-on. You can text "cut the power" from anywhere. You cannot text it back on. That takes you, at the bench, after a clean check.

Hardware

Small parts, one job each.

Breadboard16 rows wired underneath to header pins. This is the watched build area.
2 × CD74HC406716-channel multiplexers. One drives a test voltage, one senses every other row.
INA219Measures voltage and current on the power path.
Power switchA logic-level MOSFET or a relay. The firmware changes in two lines.
TemperatureA thermistor next to the parts.
ArduinoRuns the scan, the switch and the cutoff.
OLEDShows PASS, SHORT, or power cut on the bench.
Limit5 V or less, set by the multiplexer chips.

What you get

Three places to find out.

MAC

The notch

A small native app sits on the real notch. It stays invisible until something needs you, then grows downward with the fault and the fix.

CLAUDE

MCP tools

check_wiring, power_on, power_off, get_status. Claude can look at your real board before it says "now connect the LED".

PHONE

iMessage

A fault texts you. Reply "is my circuit safe?" or "cut the power". You walked away from the bench. The board did not.
